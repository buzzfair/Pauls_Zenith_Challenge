## 🎯 CORE INSIGHT

Ethical, high-converting sales conversations follow the same three-act structure as medical consultations: **Consultation** (listen to symptoms), **Diagnosis** (understand the root cause), **Prescription** (provide the cure). This theatrical framework transforms salespeople from product-pushers into trusted advisors, making objections disappear and close rates soar.

**Model Type:** HOW - Process/Strategy Overview

**Key Components:**

1. **Act 1 - Consultation (The Pour):** Your only job is to listen. The client "pours" their challenges, fears, goals, and frustrations into you while you take structured notes in your handbook. You mirror their tone, speed, and emotion. You ask clarifying questions but never jump to solutions. This builds trust and collects raw material for diagnosis.

2. **Act 2 - Diagnosis (The Investigation):** Using question funneling (4 levels deep), you probe beneath surface statements to quantify pain, understand emotional/financial impact, and map everything into 3 Pillars (Pain, Financial, Simplicity). You become a detective, asking "missing piece" questions to fill gaps. You confirm understanding using their exact language.

3. **Act 3 - Prescription (The Solution):** You play back their diagnosis using their own words, creating "they read my mind" moments. You prescribe solutions that address all 3 pillars simultaneously, pre-handling objections before they form. You deliver investment with downward tonality and authority. You guide to next steps with confidence.

**Component Relationships:**

Each act builds on the previous. **Consultation** without diagnosis = shallow relationship with no insight. **Diagnosis** without consultation = assumptions and offense. **Prescription** without the first two = pushy pitch that triggers resistance. The magic happens when all three flow seamlessly, creating a transformational conversation where the client sells themselves.

- **First reveal:** Show just Act 1 - "Most salespeople skip this entirely and jump straight to pitching."
- **Second reveal:** Add Act 2 - "The best salespeople listen AND ask deep questions, but then..."
- **Third reveal:** Complete with Act 3 - "...they prescribe with surgical precision because they've done the diagnosis."

Picture this: You walk into the doctor's office feeling uncertain, maybe defensive about your symptoms. The doctor doesn't immediately pull out a prescription pad and start writing before you've even sat down. That would be malpractice. Instead, they *listen*—really listen—as you describe what's wrong. They lean in. They take notes. They mirror your concern with their facial expressions. You feel heard.

Then they start asking questions. Not surface questions, but the kind that make you think: "How long has this been happening? What makes it worse? What have you tried? You realize they're building a complete picture, connecting dots you didn't even know existed.

Finally, after this thorough examination, they sit back and say: "Based on everything you've told me, here's what I'm seeing..." They play back your symptoms in a way that makes you think, "Yes! Exactly!" Then they prescribe—not randomly, but with surgical precision. The prescription addresses your pain, fits your lifestyle (simplicity), and works within your constraints (financial). You leave feeling certain, not sold.

**That's CDP.** You're not a salesperson; you're a sales doctor. And patients don't object to doctors who've diagnosed them correctly.

### CASE STUDY (Left-Brain Concrete)

**TechSolutions Ltd: From 22% to 61% Close Rate in 90 Days**

#### Starting Situation

- **Who:** B2B SaaS company selling project management software, 12-person sales team, enterprise deals ($50K-$200K ACV)

- **When:** Struggling for 18 months, tried three different sales training programs
- **Stakes:** Burn rate unsustainable, investors pressuring for results, considering replacing entire sales team

#### Specific Challenge

- **What They Tried Before:** Traditional SPIN selling, challenger sale methodology, extensive product demos
- **Why It Didn't Work:** Reps were jumping to 45-minute product demos after 5 minutes of discovery. Prospects felt "pitched to" rather than understood. Objections came at the end: "too expensive," "need to think about it," "let me talk to my team."
- **Gap:** No systematic diagnosis. Reps assumed they knew the pain points based on industry templates.

#### Solution Applied

- **Framework Used:** CDP Three-Act Structure
- **Implementation Process:**
    - **Week 1-2:** Retrained team on Act 1 (Consultation) - taught handbook note-taking system, mirroring techniques, and "the pour." Reps practiced 30-minute consultation role-plays with NO pitching allowed.
    - **Week 3-4:** Introduced Act 2 (Diagnosis) - trained question funneling (4 levels deep), 3-Pillar pain mapping, safety questions, and "missing piece" questions. Role-played diagnosis until reps could quantify impact and get numbers.
    - **Week 5-6:** Added Act 3 (Prescription) - taught playback technique using client's exact language, pre-objection handling, investment delivery with authority, and confident close. Full end-to-end CDP call simulations.
    - **Week 7-12:** Live implementation with call reviews, coaching sessions twice weekly, celebration of wins.
- **Timeline:** 6 weeks training, 6 weeks coaching reinforcement

#### Results (Specific Metrics)

- **Primary Outcome:** Close rate increased from 22% to 61% (178% improvement)
- **Secondary Benefits:**
  - Average sales cycle decreased from 47 days to 31 days (34% faster)
  - "I need to think about it" objection dropped from 68% of calls to 12%
  - Average deal size increased 23% ($62K → $76K ACV) due to better diagnosis revealing larger pain
  - Sales team morale transformed (attrition stopped, team grew to 18 reps)
- **Timeframe:** Results visible by week 8, sustained for 18+ months

#### ROI Analysis

- **Financial Impact:**
  - Revenue increase: $2.4M additional ARR in first year
  - Training investment: $47K (external consulting + internal time)
  - ROI: 5,000%+ in year one
- **Time Impact:** Reps spending 60% less time chasing unqualified leads, 40% more time with qualified prospects
- **Qualitative Impact:** CEO quote: "Our reps went from order-takers hoping for a yes to doctors confidently diagnosing. The energy in the sales room completely shifted."

#### Key Takeaway

The company's product hadn't changed. The pricing hadn't changed. What changed was **the sequence**: diagnosis before prescription. When prospects felt truly understood (Acts 1 & 2), they pre-sold themselves before the prescription was even delivered (Act 3). The objections disappeared because they were pre-handled during diagnosis.

### STORY (Right-Brain Concrete)

**Story Title:** "The £47 Mistake That Changed Everything"

**Story Type:**
- [x] The Icon's Story (Emily Ratcliffe - safest, most powerful)

#### Story Arc

**Characters:** Emily Ratcliffe, early in her sales career, working for a high-ticket coaching company in London. Confident, articulate, trained in "pitch-perfect" product presentations. A prospect named David, CEO of a manufacturing company, clearly frustrated but guarded on the phone.

**Setting:** A Tuesday morning Zoom call. Emily has her slide deck loaded, rehearsed her value proposition three times that morning. She's ready to crush this demo. David's video is off. She can hear typing in the background. He sounds distracted.

**Conflict:** Emily launches into her presentation. Slide 3: "Our Proven Framework." Slide 7: "ROI Calculator." Slide 12: "Case Studies." She's delivering it flawlessly. But David is silent. No questions. No excitement. Just... silence.

Finally, 22 minutes in, he interrupts: "Look, I'm sure this is great, but I don't think it's for us. Just send me the pricing and I'll review with my team." Classic objection.

Emily pivots to objection-handling scripts. She reframes. She storytells. She handles. David gets quieter. Eventually: "I appreciate your time. I'll be in touch." *Click.*

The proposal? £47,000. Her commission? Would have been £4,700. Gone.

**Turning Point:** Emily's manager listens to the call recording. Instead of criticizing the pitch, he asks one question: "What did you learn about David's actual problem?"

Emily freezes. She realizes she has no idea. She knew his industry. His company size. His job title. But his *actual* pain? His biggest fear? What keeps him up at night? What he's already tried?

Nothing.

She'd pitched a solution to a problem she'd never diagnosed.

Her manager says: "You're not a salesperson. You're a doctor. And doctors don't walk into the examination room with a prescription already written. That's malpractice."

That week, Emily completely rebuilt her approach. Next call, she did something radical: she shut up for 30 minutes. She just listened. Asked questions. Took notes. Mirrored. When the prospect finally asked, "So what do you offer?"—she asked three more questions before answering.

**Resolution:** The close rate on her next 10 calls: 70%. Her monthly commission doubled. Within a year, she became the #1 performer on a 40-person sales team. That framework she built? She called it CDP: Consultation, Diagnosis, Prescription.

The £47K loss was the tuition for a methodology that's now generated over £50M in sales for her clients.

**Social Object:** Emily still has David's email in her "lessons" folder. Subject line: "Not interested." She looks at it before big training sessions to remember: *diagnosis before prescription*.

### Learning Activity

**Activity Name:** "The Diagnosis Challenge"

**Purpose:** Experience the difference between pitch-first vs. CDP-structured conversations

**Format:**
- [x] Pairs discussion (15-20 min)

**Time Required:** 20 minutes

**Materials Needed:**
- [x] Scenario cards (provided below)
- [x] Three-act note-taking template
- [x] Timer

**Setup:** Pair up participants. Assign roles: Client (has a problem) and Salesperson.

**Instructions:**

**Step 1: The Pitch-First Approach** (5 minutes)
- Client receives scenario card with their "pain" (provided to them only)
- Salesperson knows nothing except the client's industry
- Timer starts: Salesperson has 5 minutes to make a sale
- Typically, salesperson asks 1-2 surface questions then launches into pitch
- Client is instructed to object at end: "I need to think about it"

**Step 2: The CDP Approach** (10 minutes)
- Same scenario, reset
- Salesperson now uses three-act structure:
  - Act 1 (3 min): Just listen, take notes, mirror
  - Act 2 (4 min): Ask question funnel (4 levels deep), map to 3 pillars
  - Act 3 (3 min): Play back, prescribe solution aligned to all 3 pillars
- Notice how objections don't appear when diagnosis is thorough

**Step 3: Debrief** (5 minutes)
- Switch roles and repeat, OR go straight to discussion

**Debrief Questions:**

1. "Clients: How did it feel different being diagnosed vs. being pitched to immediately?"
2. "Salespeople: What did you learn in Act 2 that you didn't know in the pitch-first version?"
3. "What would have happened if you'd prescribed without diagnosing?"

**Facilitator Notes:**

- **Common challenge:** Salespeople rush through Act 1-2 to get to prescription. Enforce time limits.
- **What to listen for:** Phrases like "I felt heard" or "I understood their problem so much better"
- **Connection back to framework:** "This is why sequence matters. Same scenario, different structure, completely different outcome."

*"You're the Head of Sales at a 50-person company. Your team's close rate has dropped 15% in the last quarter. You've tried new CRM features, updated scripts, and hired a sales trainer—nothing worked. You're frustrated and skeptical of more 'solutions.' Your CEO is pressuring you for results. ## APPLICATION INSTRUCTIONS

**How To Use The CDP Three-Act Structure™ (Step-by-Step):**

### Step 1: Prepare Your Handbook (Before the Call)

**What to do:** Set up your three-act note-taking template with sections for: Pre-appointment info, Consultation notes (left page), Diagnosis mapping (3 pillars), Prescription playback (right page)

**Ask yourself:** Do I have at least 10 minutes before this call to prepare?

**Look for:** Client's name, company info, any pre-call research that tells you their industry/role

**Time:** 10 minutes pre-call

**Output:** Clean handbook ready to capture their "pour"

---

### Step 2: Execute Act 1 - Consultation (The Pour)

**What to do:**
- Open: "I've looked over your info, but the best place to start is from the top. Tell me a bit more about [company/situation] and what's going on with [pain point]?"
- Listen for 20-30 minutes while taking structured notes
- Mirror their tone, speed, posture, emotion
- Ask clarifying questions ONLY ("What did you mean by that?" "Tell me more about...")
- Resist all urges to pitch, solve, or demonstrate

**Use the visual to:** Remind yourself you're still in Act 1—stay in listening mode

**Determine:** What are they pouring out? Pain points? Goals? Fears? Frustrations? (Just capture, don't categorize yet)

**Warning signs:**
- You're talking more than them
- You've started pitching solutions
- You're thinking about your product instead of their words

**Output:** 2-3 pages of notes in their exact language, rapport established, they feel heard

**Time:** 20-30 minutes (40% of call)

---

### Step 3: Execute Act 2 - Diagnosis (The Investigation)

**What to do:**
- Ask permission: "I've been taking notes—do I have permission to dive in with some probing questions for the next 10-15 minutes?"
- Use question funneling (4 levels): Explore pain → Explore impact → Quantify impact → Understand desire
- Map everything into 3 Pillars: Pain/Problem, Financial, Simplicity
- Ask "missing piece" questions to fill gaps in your handbook
- Confirm understanding: "So just to ensure I've understood, X is causing Y, and once we solve that, it'll allow you to Z. Is that right?"

**Use the visual to:** Check that you're going 4 levels deep (not stopping at level 1-2)

**Determine:**
- Can I quantify this pain? (Numbers, time, money)
- Do I understand the emotional/professional impact?
- Could I explain this to someone else confidently?

**Warning signs:**
- Can't put numbers to their pain
- Don't know why this matters to them personally
- Have gaps in your 3-pillar map (missing Financial or Simplicity needs)

**Output:** Complete diagnosis across all 3 pillars, quantified pain, confirmed understanding, trust deepened

**Time:** 20-30 minutes (40% of call)

---

### Step 4: Execute Act 3 - Prescription (The Solution)

**What to do:**
- Playback their diagnosis using their exact words: "So based on what you've told me, I'm seeing three key challenges..."
- Address all 3 pillars in your solution: Pain (how you solve it), Financial (how it fits their budget/ROI), Simplicity (how easy it is)
- Pre-handle objections you identified during diagnosis ("You mentioned you've been let down before, which is why we offer a guarantee...")
- Deliver investment with authority: Write it down, say "investment," downward tone, state it confidently
- Guide to next steps: "Here's what happens next..."

**Use the visual to:** Ensure you've completed Acts 1-2 before prescribing (don't skip diagnosis!)

**Determine:** Have they had a "they read my mind" moment during playback? Are they nodding/agreeing?

**Warning signs:**
- They look confused (you didn't confirm understanding in Act 2)
- Objections arise (you missed something in diagnosis)
- They're asking for "time to think" (you haven't addressed all 3 pillars)

**Output:** Confident prescription aligned to their diagnosis, investment delivered, next steps clear, objections pre-handled

**Time:** 10-15 minutes (20% of call)

---

### Step 5: Close or Extend

**What to do:**
- If buying decision is ready: Complete the close ("Let's get you started...")
- If more stakeholders involved: Secure next steps with all decision-makers ("How do we get [partner/boss] on a call?")
- If real objection emerges: Go back to diagnosis ("Help me understand what I've missed...")

**Output:** Either a closed deal, a scheduled next-step call with decision-makers, or clarity on what's blocking them

**Time:** 5-10 minutes

---

- Understanding of question funneling (see Framework #4)

- Product knowledge (so you can prescribe confidently in Act 3)

**Ongoing Use:** Every sales call, every time. CDP is not a tactic—it's THE structure.
